# How Pro ATC works

What the controllers do, in detail. For installing and a first flight, see the [README](../README.md).

Pro ATC does not use X-Plane's own ATC. It reads the aircraft's state and the sim's data files, and everything a controller says is decided by its own rules engine. The language model only interprets what the pilot said (`intents.py`); replies are built from templates, so runways, routes, levels, squawks and frequencies are never invented.

With a flight plan loaded (from SimBrief, or a destination and cruise level typed in):

| Step | Controller | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Clearance | Delivery | Destination, the SID that fits your route and runway, initial altitude from the SID, squawk |
| Push, taxi | Ground | Push and start, a taxi route over the airport's real taxiways, a clearance for each runway crossed on the way |
| Takeoff | Tower | Takeoff clearance with the wind, then a hand-off once airborne |
| Climb | Departure | Identified, step climbs up to FL240 or your cruise level, hand-off to the centre whose airspace you are in |
| Cruise | Centres | Climb to cruise, hand-offs as you cross sector boundaries, step descents starting at the right distance, with the STAR |
| Arrival | Approach | Descent, radar vectors that depend on where you come from, speed control, clearance for the best published approach or the one you ask for |
| Landing | Tower, Ground | Landing clearance, vacate, taxi to a stand that fits your aircraft and airline |

Readbacks are checked at every step: a wrong runway, squawk, frequency, pressure, level or heading gets "negative" and the instruction again. Procedures come from X-Plane's CIFP data (`procedures.py`; Navigraph data in `Custom Data` is used when present), centres and their boundaries from `atc.dat` (`centers.py`), the vectoring pattern is in `vectors.py` and the radar controllers' logic in `ifr.py`. VFR circuits are covered too. Speech that matches no call the engine knows goes to the language model, which may answer with information, "standby" or "unable" only: a reply that reads like a clearance or an instruction is not spoken, because the engine would not know about it. A call you click needs no language model at all.

What the controllers also do: climbs and descents come in steps of about 10,000 ft, the next one as you approach your cleared level; approach gives speed control (220, 180, then 160 knots to four miles) and your range from touchdown with the approach clearance; tower says "continue approach" to an arrival still far out and clears it to land inside four and a half miles; a go-around sends you back to approach for new vectors; a direct routing to a waypoint on your filed route is granted on request. They also watch you: a level bust, an assigned heading not being flown, the wrong squawk on first contact, and an instruction nobody read back (repeated once after 30 seconds). An airline flight number filed in SimBrief (for example BAW123) is spoken as "Speedbird one two three"; otherwise the registration is used.

Arrivals follow the STAR: its level restrictions are issued one by one ("descend flight level two six zero, level by Inpip") at the distance a three degree path needs, and you are told if you cross a waypoint high. Approach can hold you at the published hold at the end of the STAR: always on request, and on its own initiative according to `[atc] holding` in `config.toml` (`never`, the default; `sometimes` for about one arrival in four; `always`); it releases you after the stated delay, or when you say you are ready. Vectors and descents respect the minimum safe altitude around the airport and the minimum off-route altitude of the terrain below (`navfix.py`), and a radar controller calls a low altitude alert if you descend below 1,000 ft above the ground away from the approach.

Weather: while X-Plane is on real weather, each airport's ATIS, runway in use and pressure come from its own METAR (`metar.py`, read from `Output/real weather`), so the destination is right from any distance; with preset or custom weather the sim's weather at the aircraft is used. The ATIS reports gusts, thunderstorms, snow, fog and low visibility procedures, a controller announces a new ATIS to an aircraft on frequency, and you can ask any controller for the wind and pressure, ask a centre for the destination weather, and request a deviation around weather.

Other aircraft: with X-Plane's AI aircraft enabled, controllers see them (`traffic.py`). Radar controllers call traffic ("traffic, two o'clock, five miles, opposite direction, one thousand feet above"), tower holds a departure for an aircraft on final and clears it when the runway is free, tells an arrival it is number two, and sends it around if the runway is still occupied at one mile. The app does not control those aircraft. Parallel runways are split between landing and departure. Mayday and pan-pan are acknowledged on any frequency and the landing is cleared at once. A VFR flight can ask a radar controller for flight following. A SID's "at or above" crossing flown low is called.

Approaches: vectors are a downwind, base and intercept from behind or abeam the airport, a 45 degree dogleg onto final for an aircraft already out on the approach side, and straight in for one nearly lined up; how far out final is joined differs from arrival to arrival, and a downwind leg is extended while another aircraft is on final. You can ask for another type of approach ("request the R NAV approach"), and "field in sight" gets a visual approach when the reported weather allows it. A runway with no approach of its own is reached by an approach to another runway and circling, in weather good enough to circle. If the runway in use changes while you are being vectored, approach says so and starts again for the new runway. A go-around flies the published missed approach to its altitude (from the CIFP data) and goes back to approach, where you can try again or ask to divert: to the alternate filed in SimBrief, or to any airport by its code. A diversion gives a heading and a level and hands you on, and the flight continues to the new destination.

On the ground: a taxi route that crosses another runway stops there ("hold short runway two seven left"). The crossing clearance comes when you report holding short, or by itself once you have stopped at the runway, and waits for an aircraft landing on it; entering the runway without it gets a word from the controller. "Request progressive taxi" gives the next taxiway and which way to turn onto it, from where you are. After landing you are sent to a stand that takes your aircraft's size, your airline's own where the scenery says which airline uses which stand.

The ATIS in the side panel: the airport's current ATIS is written down row by row (`atis.decoded`): information letter and time, runways for landing and departure, wind and gusts, visibility, weather, cloud layers, temperature and dew point, and the altimeter or QNH, in the units the recording uses. Rows that matter for the approach (low visibility, a low ceiling, a thunderstorm, low visibility procedures) are in red. It is the same information as the recording, and it is shown for the destination too once it is within 80 miles.

Two radios: the app follows the aircraft's audio panel. The COM radio selected for transmitting is the one you work, as before. The other one is listened to as well when its reception is on and its volume is up: an ATIS tuned on it plays under whatever happens on the first radio, at half the loudness, lower still if you turn it down in the cockpit (`SecondRadio` in `radio.py`). It is listening only: a controller on the second radio is not talking to you and says nothing. The side panel shows the second radio under the tuned one, and each frequency in the list has a small button to tune it there instead. "Listen to the second radio" in Settings switches it off.

Who answers on a frequency: around a large city several airports list the same approach frequency, a centre and an approach controller can share one, and two centres can too. The rule is the same everywhere (`radio.station_on`, `navdata.AirportDB.station`): a station is heard within its range and below its ceiling (a tower is not heard by an airliner in the cruise); the station you are talking to keeps answering; when you are handed to a station, that station answers; and on a shared frequency the airports of your flight plan come before a nearer one. Approach frequencies labelled by sector ("APP (091-240)") are given for the side of the airport you are on. Runways are matched to procedures whether the airport writes "1C" or "01C".

Between centres: the next centre is given about a minute and a half before the boundary, and just across a boundary the last centre still answers and hands over, so a missed hand-off does not leave the frequency silent. In the air the side panel always shows the centre for where the aircraft is, and, when no airport's station is tuned, the frequencies of the airport nearby or of the destination within 80 miles. In the descent, each next level waits for the distance as well as for the aircraft nearing its cleared level.

Saying it properly: type what you want in your own words, in any language ("I want to go to the runway", "quero taxiar para a pista"), and press "Phraseology". The entry box then holds the proper call, to read aloud or to send. Nothing is transmitted until you do. The calls that fit the flight right now come from the rules engine, already worded; the language model only picks the one that says what you want (`phrasing.py`), and that call is sent with its meaning attached, like a button. A request that is not among them ("I need two more minutes", "smoke in the cabin") is worded by the model and then goes to the controller like anything typed. Without a language model, the nearest button is used.

The airport map: on the ground the window shows the airport from above, north up, drawn from the sim's own airport file: pavement, runways, taxiway centrelines and names, stands (`chart.py`). After a taxi clearance the route you were given is highlighted from the aircraft to where you must stop. A runway to hold short of is marked in red, and the route beyond it is dashed until you are cleared to cross. The line shortens as you taxi; more than 250 metres off it, the way is shown again from where you are. "Route" fits the whole route, "Follow" keeps the aircraft in the middle, and the map can be dragged and zoomed. It is the same route the controller spoke: the map decides nothing. Curved pavement edges are drawn as straight lines between their points, and an airport whose scenery has no taxiway network shows its runways and pavement only.

Voices and chatter: each controller has a voice of their own and keeps it (`voices.py`), British in the United Kingdom and Ireland, American in the United States and Canada. There are two voice engines (`tts.py`), chosen in Settings. Kokoro is the default: it makes a whole sentence before any of it is played. Phonon is on trial: it hands over sound while it is still working, so the controller starts speaking a fraction of a second after the reply is known, and the sound is played and put through the radio effect piece by piece (`Player.play_stream`, `radio_stream`). Phonon's model (65 MB, Apache 2.0) and each of its voices are downloaded the first time they are used. A voice chosen for one engine becomes one of the same accent and sex in the other. Chatter can be switched off in Settings; an exchange that is playing stops at once. With radio chatter on (`[atc] chatter`: `off`, `some`, `busy`) you hear other flights being worked on the frequency (`chatter.py`). That is sound only: those aircraft are not in the sim and the controller does not sequence you against them. It uses the real runway in use, wind, taxiways and frequencies, pauses while a controller is waiting for your readback, and stops the moment you transmit.

Visual flying (`vfr.py`, `airspace.py`):

- **Airfields with no controller** (CTAF, UNICOM, "Traffic"): you announce yourself and nobody answers, as in real life. The app builds the calls from where you are ("Santa Paula traffic, Cessna ..., nine miles south, inbound for landing runway two two, Santa Paula"). A UNICOM operator gives an airport advisory when asked.
- **Information services** (AFIS, "Information", "Radio"): the runway in use, wind, pressure and known traffic, and "take off at your discretion" / "land at your discretion". Never a clearance.
- **Towers**: how to join the circuit depends on where you come from (straight in, base, downwind, or across the field first) and on the circuit direction the airport publishes; "number two, follow the traffic on final"; circuits ("closed traffic" in the US); a low approach; a VFR departure in a direction, with "frequency change approved" once you have left the zone.
- **Control zones** come from the sim's own airspace file, which covers the world: clearance to enter, a zone transit ("cleared to transit the control zone, not above ...", or "transition approved" / a class Bravo clearance in the US), and release when you leave. A towered airport missing from that file gets a five mile circle.
- **Special VFR** when the reported weather is below VFR minima: the tower says so and clears special VFR if you ask ("the field is IFR, say intentions" in the US).
- **Radar services** are what the country calls them: flight following in the US and Canada, a Basic or Traffic Service in the United Kingdom (no traffic information on a Basic Service), a flight information service elsewhere. Under any of them you are told before you fly into a control zone, class B, C or D airspace, or a restricted, prohibited or danger area, with the tower to call for entry.
- The app shows the airspace you are in.

Not covered: named VFR reporting points and published VFR routes (the sim's data does not have them), flight service stations and "Information" en-route frequencies, helicopter routes, and whether a restricted area is active (every one is treated as active).

In the app: "Debrief" reviews the flight's radio work from the log (readbacks corrected or missed, level busts, runway incursions, restrictions missed, calls on a dead frequency) with a score and, with a language model, a few sentences from the instructor (`debrief.py`); it is also written into the saved log. The waypoints, procedures and airports of your flight plan are given to the speech recogniser and to the model that interprets you, so "BIBAX" is heard as a waypoint rather than as two words. The language model can run on your own machine instead of OpenRouter: Settings, Language model, "Local model" takes any OpenAI-compatible address such as Ollama or LM Studio, needs no key and costs nothing (a small local model understands less than Claude Haiku does; clicked calls need no model at all). And the app's screen can be opened on a phone or tablet on the same network: Settings, Phone or tablet shows an address and a pairing code. The microphone stays the computer's; the device shows the conversation and sends clicked and typed calls. It is off by default, asks for the code, refuses a device that keeps guessing, and cannot change the key or the model.

Also in the app: a settings screen (microphone, speakers, push-to-talk on a keyboard key or a joystick or yoke button, voices, model, holding, traffic); a copilot that reads instructions back, or also tunes hand-off frequencies and checks in with the next controller; an instructor that explains the last instruction and tells you what to say next; "Save log", which writes the flight's radio work to a transcript and a JSON file for bug reports; and what the session has spent on OpenRouter, in the header.

Known simplifications: the sim's other aircraft are seen but not controlled, so there is no sequencing between them; an R NAV approach is flown from vectors to final like an I L S, not from its initial fix; a circling approach is cleared but the circling itself is not watched; station names are whatever X-Plane's data calls them.

The radio: a controller answers only when the tuned frequency belongs to a station in range (ground and delivery 10 NM, tower 30 NM, approach and departure 60 NM), and answers as that station. Tune an ATIS frequency within 60 NM and the ATIS plays in a loop. Without a sim connection the controller talks freely, so the audio chain can be tested.

Procedures as published (`procedures.py`, `restrictions.py`): a SID is read as its part for your runway, the part common to all runways, and the en-route transition that ends on your filed route; a STAR as the transition that starts on your route, the common part, and the part for the landing runway. Only the restrictions on the legs you fly are used. Every kind counts: at, at or above, at or below, between two levels, and speed limits. On a SID you are told if you cross a fix too low, too high or too fast. On a STAR each restriction is issued in turn with its speed ("descend flight level two zero zero, level by Delta, speed two eight zero knots or less at Delta"). If you leave the track of your SID or STAR for more than a moment, without a direct routing or a weather deviation, the controller asks you to confirm you are following it.

RNAV approaches are flown as procedures, not vectored: when the approach has a published initial approach fix that is still ahead of you, you are cleared direct to it and for the approach "via" that fix, at its crossing altitude. Otherwise, and for an ILS, you get vectors. With no approach to the runway in use, the controller tries in order: circling from another runway's approach, landing straight in from that approach if the tailwind is small, a visual approach if the weather allows one, and otherwise tells you there is no approach available and asks your intentions; a diversion or a hold is offered.

What can be asked, on any frequency: to confirm or say again one item (the cleared level, heading, speed, squawk, runway, frequency, pressure, clearance limit, taxi route), answered from what the controller actually assigned, or "no heading assigned" when there is none; the runway in use, the approach to expect and the distance to touchdown (a centre answers for the destination from its weather, or says which approach controller will); the ATIS letter and a wind check. "Unable" to an instruction gets it cancelled or replaced ("roger, maintain flight level one two zero, advise when able"; at the runway, the takeoff clearance cancelled). A level can be asked of departure, approach and every centre; a different runway is given when it exists and the wind allows it (with a new taxi route, or a new approach) and otherwise declined with the reason; "not ready", a return to the stand, a return to the departure airport once airborne, and a frequency change (an instrument flight is asked to report back) are answered too. "Roger", "thank you" and "good day" get silence, and a check-in after first contact gets "roger". When no rule covers a call, the controller says who to call for it ("contact Westfield Departure one two five decimal five for higher") or "unable" and what: the language model's free-form reply is left for speech nobody foresaw (`answers.py`).

Readbacks: a wrong item is corrected. An item left out (the runway, the squawk, the level, the heading, the frequency, the runway to hold short of) is asked for once ("read back the runway"); a second incomplete readback is accepted, because speech recognition does drop words. "Roger" alone is not a readback.

Safety checks that hold for every instruction: a level you ask for is never given below the terrain; the runway is watched after you are cleared to land or to take off, and the clearance is withdrawn if something else is on it; no visual approach is given in weather that does not allow one.

Airports come from your custom scenery first, in the order of `scenery_packs.ini`, as the sim itself chooses them: an add-on airport's taxiways, stands and frequencies replace the default ones. While the sim is paused, controllers and chatter are silent and the clocks for holds and reminders stop. A new flight starts clean when you load a plan after a flight, ask for a clearance after landing and parking, move the aircraft in the sim, or click "new flight".

The whole route: airways are read from the sim's `earth_awy.dat` (`airways.py`), so "ALFAX UL9 BRAVO" is known waypoint by waypoint. A direct is given to any waypoint ahead on the route, the waypoint names are passed to speech recognition, and in the cruise a flight more than ten miles off its filed route for two minutes is asked to confirm its routing. Latitude and longitude waypoints (`53N020W`) are understood.

Oceanic airspace: in a sector the sim's data names as oceanic (Shanwick, Gander, New York Oceanic and others) there is no radar. The station is "Radio", nothing is "identified", no level bust or traffic is called, and the controller takes position reports ("roger, maintain flight level three six zero, report five three north zero two zero west") and clears level changes with "report reaching". Oceanic clearances with tracks and Mach numbers are not issued.

A pressure below 1000 is said with its unit ("Q N H niner niner eight hectopascals"), as the rules require, so that it is not taken for inches.

Still simplified: other aircraft are seen but not controlled; oceanic tracks, Mach number technique and SELCAL are not modelled; approach minima are the pilot's to judge (the controller does not refuse an approach for weather); a circling approach is cleared but the circling is not watched; airways between route waypoints are not followed, so the route is watched on the SID and the STAR only.
